# NepaFact: Multilingual Factuality and Robustness Benchmark for Nepali AI

## Abstract

NepaFact tests whether AI systems give reliable Nepal-focused answers when questions are written in English, Nepali, and Romanized Nepali and when users introduce false assumptions, social pressure, or insufficient evidence. The initial release contains 25 source-grounded facts expanded into 100 controlled items and three language forms (300 question presentations).

## Research questions

1. How does factuality change across English, Nepali, and Romanized Nepali?
2. Do models correct false assumptions instead of accepting them?
3. Do models abstain when a claim cannot be established from the supplied evidence?
4. Are citations both present and actually relevant to the answer?

## Dataset

The dataset is generated by `data/questions_seed.py` and exported to `data/questions.jsonl`. Each record contains a language form, script, domain, controlled error category, gold answer, expected evidence, answerability flag, and trusted source. The four categories are `direct_fact`, `false_assumption`, `pressure_to_conform`, and `insufficient_evidence`.

## Evaluation

The baseline evaluator reports factuality, citation correctness, abstention quality, evidence grounding, and hallucination rate. Keyword matching is intentionally transparent and conservative. It is not a substitute for expert adjudication.

For the paper-quality run, sample at least 20% of responses stratified by model, language, and category. Two human evaluators should label factuality, citation entailment, and whether abstention was appropriate. Report Cohen's kappa or Krippendorff's alpha, disagreements, and adjudication rules.

## Experimental protocol

Record model name/version, system prompt, temperature, retrieval configuration, date, and response file hash. Use identical question order and no model-specific post-processing. For health and law items, do not present benchmark answers as personal advice.

## Limitations and future work

- The seed sources should be periodically revalidated, especially policy, health, and agricultural statistics.
- Automatic keyword scoring can miss paraphrases and may over-credit answers containing a keyword.
- Romanized Nepali has spelling variation; future releases should add multiple crowd-checked transliterations per item.
- Human evaluation and source-level entailment are needed for strong publication claims.
